# Outcome based performance reviews

I’ve struggled to deliver performance reviews in a way I felt was really 
effective. They always focused too much on the negative. Here I present a format that 
tries to rectify that.

Many performance and promotion reviews take the form:

Formal rating

Part 1: Strengths
Part 2: Weakness/Opportunity
Part 3: action/growth plan

Conversations in this format encourage the wrong focus. The focus tends to be on the 
opportunities. I believe this because the format is 
translated by the individual as; these are my weaknesses as a person. 
This interpretation implicitly ties the review to the person’s identity, which then causes a slew of 
problems. Principal among them is the inability to hear certain feedback because they 
are taking it personally. Re-naming weaknesses as opportunities is not enough to break 
this mental tie in the team member’s mind.  

Another issue with the strengths and opportunities format is (in my experience) 
staying in the negative and having one negative discussion after another (even if its 
framed as a positive opportunity) starts to weather the team member's energy 
in the conversation. 

To break this mental tie between opportunities, weakness and identity, we have to use a different structure.

The structure I have started using is to divide the review into three sections:

* Demonstrated Outcomes
* High level goals
* Aspirational outcomes

Here is how I define each of these.

Demonstrated Outcomes: 

Is an outcome that the person has contributed to or solely created. Some examples can 
be, technical lead on feature X, delivered it in 2 weeks resulting in a 15% increase 
in revenue measured by whatever. It can also be; presented on a 
community of practice meeting for 500 engineers on the topic of fault tolerant 
recommendation engines.

High Level Goals:

These are the growth opportunities we want to go after collectively, manager and team 
member. They can originate from weaknesses or strengths.

Aspirational outcomes: 

Based on the goals and the current outcomes the manager and team member should define 
aspirational outcomes to help prove we have reached the high level goals. I think of this like test driven development for your career.

## New Outcome based format
Here is an illustrative example. (It is not drawn from an actual review, but is based on recurring themes I’ve observed in practice. I added some contrived numbers to make it feel less abstract).

### Demonstrated Outcomes

#### Delivery Excellence
- Developed a high-throughput event processing pipeline handling 1B+ events/day ahead of schedule 
- Delivered project Y behind schedule and with 3 production incidents caused by the launch
- Drove architecture, refinement and delivery for project X that added $50 million in additional revenue. 

#### Influence & leadership
- Secured executive buy-in for major platform modernization initiative  
- Mentored 10+ engineers, resulting in multiple promotions within the team  
- Lost psychological safety in a refinement meeting you were leading
- Championed adoption of TypeScript across the org, reducing runtime bugs by 30%  
- Lead project X, which delivered on time, however feedback from the team involved was very negative (low developer experience scored)

#### Tech Depth-Breadth
- Architectural proposals for data product pipelines were rejected by the architecture bar
- Implemented zero-downtime deployments with Kubernetes across regions  
- Drove cross-org alignment on API standards across 5 business units  
- Co-authored whitepaper on scalable ML inference for internal use  

## High level goals:
- Have Staff engineer level delivery impact
- Increase teams delivery velocity
- Decrease production incidents in your initiatives 
- Demonstrate senior level contributions to machine learning systems
- Build executive presence and ability to influence senior leadership 

### Aspirational Outcomes

1. **Staff-level Delivery Impact**  
   Lead end-to-end delivery of a multi-team initiative (e.g., cross-org platform migration or a revenue-generating product feature) that contributes **$25M+ in measurable business value**, with recognition from senior leadership.  

2. **Increase Team Delivery Velocity**  
   Implement engineering process improvements (e.g., backlog refinement, CI/CD pipeline automation, or dependency management) that result in a **20–30% reduction in cycle time** across the team, as measured by DORA metrics.  

3. **Decrease Production Incidents**  
   Decrease the change failure rate in your next 3 projects to less than 15%. Note change failure rate is a DORA metric measured by (number of deployments that result in production incidents or need a rollback) / (total number of deployments).  

4. **Senior-level ML Contributions**  
   Drive technical design and implementation of a **machine learning system deployed at scale** (e.g., inference pipeline, personalization model, or data product) that passes architecture review and becomes a critical dependency for a business units.  

5. **Executive Presence & Influence**  
   Present and secure **executive buy-in** for a strategic technology investment or platform modernization proposal, influencing resourcing decisions at the VP+ level.  

## Concluding thoughts
The reason I like this is the positive and negative outcomes have the same procedure 
applied to them. It gives the individual a nice mental break between positive and 
negative. This break allows for energy levels and overall optimism in the conversation 
to stay high, even when having really tough conversations about performance issues. It 
also breaks the mapping to the persons identity (we are talking about specific 
outcomes not the strengths and weaknesses of the individual). This let’s both manager 
and team member have useful discussions on the merits of the outcome and why it matters 
(good or bad).

Regardless of the format it is important to make sure the team member understands:

1. The outcomes are not who they are as a person
2. The outcomes are not what they are capable of producing in the future. We all are 
much more capable than any one snapshot in time (eg. at the end of the year review). 
We all get better with time.
3. The outcomes are not completely representative of what they are capable of even at 
the current time. Those outcomes are just a small piece of a larger space. That piece 
is the fraction of outcomes that their peers, managers and the organization 
happen to see and focus on. That piece is not just constrained by the team members 
capabilities. It is in large part determined by the opportunities the manager and the 
business road map is able to give to the individual. So this view could be very 
different from what the team member is capable of, given different technological and 
business circumstances.
