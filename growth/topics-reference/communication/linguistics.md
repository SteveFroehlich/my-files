# Linguistics study

## Linguistics
Linguistics is the scientific study of language and its structure. It systematically analyzes how human languages are constructed, how they are acquired by speakers, how they function within cultures, and how they evolve over time.

## Semantics
Semantics is the formal branch of linguistics dedicated to the objective, scientific study of literal meaning in language. Within the broader field of linguistics, semantics occupies a central position alongside syntax (the rules of sentence structure) and pragmatics (the study of meaning derived from social context). Specifically, semantics bridges the gap between how individual words are formed (morphology) and how human brains decode those words into structured, logical thoughts.

The everyday cultural use of "semantics"—which often frames the word as a complaint about petty, pedantic word choices—muddies the scientific definition by confusing an objective study of meaning with a deliberate evasion of reality. To resolve this confusion, we separated the word into two distinct domains: Linguistic Semantics (the literal, scientific analysis of language structures) and Cultural Semantics (the rhetorical focus on superficial labels over substance).
