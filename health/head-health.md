
# How to increase serotonin and norepinephrine
### 1. Physical Activity
Movement is one of the most effective ways to boost both chemicals simultaneously.
*   **Aerobic Exercise**: Activities like running, swimming, or cycling increase the firing rates of serotonin neurons. This leads to the "runner's high" where serotonin and endorphins rise.
*   **Strength Training**: Incorporating weights or bodyweight exercises can specifically improve metabolism and increase norepinephrine levels.
*   **Intensity**: Even 20–30 minutes of moderate exercise can stabilize norepinephrine levels and increase the sensitivity of its receptors.

### 2. Dietary Building Blocks
Your brain requires specific amino acids to manufacture these neurotransmitters.
*   **For Serotonin (Tryptophan)**: Tryptophan is the essential precursor to serotonin. You can find it in turkey, eggs, cheese, salmon, tofu, and nuts. Pairing these with complex carbohydrates (like oats or whole grains) helps the tryptophan reach your brain more effectively.
*   **For Norepinephrine (Tyrosine/Phenylalanine)**: These amino acids are the precursors for both dopamine and norepinephrine. High-protein foods like poultry, fish, lean meats, beans, and dairy are excellent sources.
*   **B Vitamins and Magnesium**: These act as "co-factors," which are necessary for the chemical conversion process. B6 and Magnesium (found in leafy greens, avocados, and seeds) are particularly critical for synthesizing these chemicals.

### 3. Environmental and Lifestyle Shifts
*   **Sunlight Exposure**: Bright morning light (10–15 minutes) stimulates the brain to produce serotonin. It also helps regulate your internal clock to release norepinephrine properly throughout the day.
* **Be around other humans**. I don’t explicitly have to interact but even just being around people at a coffee shop makes me feel better in the morning 
*   **Cold Exposure**: Short bursts of cold, such as a one-minute cold shower, can cause a significant acute spike in norepinephrine levels, increasing alertness and mood.
*   **Quality Sleep**: Your brain restores neurotransmitter levels during deep sleep. Chronic sleep deprivation can overstimulate the stress response and deplete norepinephrine.
*   **Stress Management**: High cortisol from chronic stress impairs serotonin receptors. Practices like meditation, massage, and deep breathing help lower stress hormones, allowing natural serotonin and norepinephrine levels to stabilize.

### 4. Natural Supplements
*      **Probiotics**: Since roughly 90–95% of the body's serotonin is produced in the gut, taking probiotics can support the gut bacteria involved in this process. Eg drink Kombucha.
*  **creatine** may increase cognitive function. Need more research on dosing 
* **5-HTP or L-Tryptophan**: Direct precursors that may help increase serotonin production.
*   **St. John's Wort**: An herbal supplement often used as a natural alternative to antidepressants; it is believed to increase both serotonin and norepinephrine activity.
